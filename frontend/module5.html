01. Quantum computing and healthcare

Healthcare research involves understanding complex biological systems, molecules and chemical interactions.

Quantum computing may eventually help researchers simulate molecular behaviour and investigate chemical systems that are difficult to model accurately with classical computers.

Possible research areas

🧬 Molecular simulation

💊 Drug discovery research

🔬 Understanding chemical interactions

These are active research areas. Quantum computers are not currently a replacement for existing medical tools or clinical testing.

02. Quantum computing and sustainability

Climate change and sustainability involve complicated problems across energy, materials, transport and industry.

Energy research

Quantum simulations may help researchers investigate materials for batteries, solar cells and energy storage.

Climate and materials

Researchers are exploring whether quantum methods can help model chemical reactions and material properties.

Optimisation

Some research explores quantum approaches to selected optimisation problems, such as planning and resource allocation.

03. Quantum computing and cybersecurity

Powerful future quantum computers could threaten some cryptographic systems that are widely used today.

Post-quantum cryptography develops cryptographic methods designed to resist attacks from quantum computers.

Why responsible preparation matters

🔐 Protecting digital information

🛡️ Preparing for future technology

🌐 Building trustworthy systems

04. Ethics and responsible innovation

Powerful technologies bring responsibilities. Quantum computing research should consider how its benefits and risks affect people and society.

Accessibility

Learning resources should be understandable and accessible to people from different educational backgrounds.

Privacy and security

Researchers and organisations should prepare for the cybersecurity implications of future quantum technologies.

Honest communication

It is important to distinguish current capabilities from future possibilities and avoid exaggerated claims.

Collaboration

Responsible progress requires researchers, educators, organisations and communities to work together.
