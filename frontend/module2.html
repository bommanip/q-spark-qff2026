01. What is superposition?

A classical bit has a definite value of either 0 or 1. A qubit can also exist in a combination of these two basis states. This is called superposition.

Meet a qubit in superposition

Q
|0⟩
+
|1⟩

A superposition is a quantum state with amplitudes for both |0⟩ and |1⟩.

When we measure the qubit, we get either 0 or 1. The probabilities depend on the qubit's state.

For an equal superposition, the probability of each result is 50%.

Important: Superposition does not mean that we simply have an ordinary bit secretly showing both values. It is a genuine quantum state.

03. What is entanglement?

Entanglement is a quantum phenomenon in which two or more qubits share a joint quantum state.

Their measurement results can be strongly correlated, even when the qubits are far apart.

A shared quantum state can connect the measurement behaviour of both qubits.

Example: In a Bell state, measuring both qubits in the same basis produces correlated results.

If we prepare the Bell state (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, the possible measurement outcomes are 00 and 11.

05. Superposition vs Entanglement

Let's understand the difference.

Superposition:

A single qubit can be in a combination of basis states.

Entanglement:

Multiple qubits share a joint quantum state that cannot be described as independent states of each qubit.
