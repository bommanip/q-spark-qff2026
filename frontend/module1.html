01. What is a computer?

💻

A computer is a machine that processes information.

Think about your mobile phone or laptop. You use it to watch videos, send messages, play games and search for information.

A computer receives information, processes it according to instructions and gives you a result.

Example: You type 5 + 5 into a calculator. It processes your input and displays 10.

02. What is a bit?

Computers represent information using tiny units called bits.

A bit is the smallest unit of information in classical computing. It can have one of two values: 0 or 1.

Meet the two possible bit values

0
1

0 = OFF

1 = ON

Imagine a simple light switch. It can be OFF or ON. This is a useful way to imagine a classical bit.

03. What is a quantum computer?

⚛️

A different way of processing information.

A quantum computer is a type of computer that uses principles of quantum physics to process information.

Ordinary computers use bits. Quantum computers use quantum bits, called qubits.

Qubits can show quantum behaviours such as superposition and entanglement.

04. Meet the qubit

Q

A qubit is the basic unit of quantum information.

Unlike a classical bit, a qubit can be prepared in a superposition of 0 and 1.

When we measure a qubit, we get a classical result: either 0 or 1.

Don't worry if superposition sounds complicated. We'll explore it visually in Module 2!
